Public-source check **complete (2026-10-17)**; the terms remain **unstated** for the files this project uses. The remaining action is outbound contact with VITO, which this environment cannot perform.

## What was checked, and what it shows

1. **Archive page** (`https://provide.marvin.vito.be/ftp/compressed_daily/`) states no licence. It asks users to cite De Ridder, Lauwaet & Maiheu (2015), doi:10.1016/j.uclim.2015.01.001, and Souverijns et al. (2026), doi:10.1016/j.dib.2026.112497. A citation request is not a licence.
2. **The bytes we use carry no licence.** Opening the cached annual files (`WBGT_year_daily_max_YYYY.nc`) shows global attributes `crs`, `title`, `institution` (VITO), `creator`, `source` — and no licence or attribution field.
3. **Copernicus sibling record** (the strongest candidate for a transferable licence): the CDS catalogue record `sis-urban-climate-cities` returns `"license": "CC-BY-4.0"` (https://cds.climate.copernicus.eu/api/catalogue/v1/collections/sis-urban-climate-cities). It is titled "Climate variables for cities in Europe from 2008 to 2017 (**Deprecated 10-05-2024**)", covers **100 cities including Brussels** for 2008–2017, and its request form enumerates hourly `air_temperature`, `wind_speed`, `specific_humidity`, `relative_humidity`, `rural_urban_mask`, `land_sea_mask` — **no WBGT**. VITO now serves the historical archive itself, which is why the portal page says the data was "previously provided by the Copernicus Climate Data Store".
4. **Decadal sibling record**: Souverijns et al. (2026) decadal 2010–2100 indicators are CC BY 4.0 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.13361538) and are a separate record.

**Conclusion:** the CC-BY-4.0 CDS licence covers the CDS record (hourly meteorological variables, 100 cities, CDS channel). Our record adds WBGT (VITO-computed), daily aggregation and further cities, and is served outside the CDS, so neither sibling licence is a grant for these bytes. Status therefore stays `unresolved`; ranking inputs unchanged; derived values exploratory only.
